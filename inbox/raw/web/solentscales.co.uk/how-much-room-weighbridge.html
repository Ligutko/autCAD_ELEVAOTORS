Planning Space for a Commercial Weighbridge Installation

  • By Solent Scales
  • 20 Mar 2023
  • 0 Comments

Weighbridges are excellent for high-volume vehicle weighing, but they require more site space than many businesses initially realise. Unlike portable axle pads that can be stored away when not in use, a weighbridge is a permanent or semi-permanent installation. Proper planning helps avoid costly modifications later.

Understanding the full space requirements - including the platform, access ramps, and vehicle manoeuvring areas - is essential for a successful installation.

Standard Weighbridge Platform Sizes

Most modular weighbridges start with a single section around 8m long by 3m wide. Larger configurations are available for bigger vehicles or higher capacities. However, the platform size alone doesn’t tell the full story.

Ramp Requirements for Surface-Mounted Systems

Surface-mounted weighbridges need approach ramps on both ends to allow vehicles to drive on and off safely. Steep ramps risk vehicles grounding or scraping, particularly longer articulated trucks.

Recommended ramp length is typically 3.5 metres on each side. This brings the total length requirement to approximately 15m x 3m for the weighbridge and ramps combined.

Vehicle Approach and Manoeuvring Space

You also need clear space for vehicles to approach the weighbridge and exit safely. For an 8m platform, allowing similar clearance at both ends ensures smooth operation without blockages. This brings the practical total space to around 31m in length.

This extra area doesn’t need to be dedicated solely to the weighbridge, it can remain accessible for other vehicles and foot traffic, but it must stay clear of permanent obstructions like buildings or fixed equipment.

Space-Saving Options

Pit-Mounted Weighbridges

Installing the weighbridge in a pit brings the platform level with the surrounding ground, eliminating the need for ramps. This can save around 7 metres of length compared to surface-mounted systems. Pit installations have different civil engineering requirements, including drainage and foundation work, but they offer a lower-profile, more compact footprint.

Reverse-Off Configurations

In very tight spaces, some businesses operate a reverse-off system where vehicles drive on and then reverse off. This removes the need for exit clearance on one side. While effective for lower-volume operations, it significantly slows throughput and is generally less efficient for busy sites.

Additional Site Planning Considerations

  • Foundations and Civil Works: Both surface and pit installations require solid, level foundations designed to handle heavy loads without settlement.
  • Access Routes: Ensure adequate turning circles and road widths leading to the weighbridge.
  • Safety and Positioning: Good visibility, lighting, and barriers help drivers position vehicles correctly.
  • Future Expansion: Modular systems allow you to add sections later, so consider potential growth when planning the initial layout.
  • Automation Equipment: Driver-operated terminals, barriers, and sensors need additional space beside the weighbridge.

Making the Right Decision for Your Site

Every business site is different. Factors like daily vehicle numbers, typical vehicle lengths, available yard space, and budget for civil works all influence the best configuration.

Working with experienced suppliers early in the planning stage helps you evaluate options and avoid common pitfalls. They can assess your site and recommend the most practical solution - whether surface-mounted, pit-mounted, or even a modular relocatable system.

At Solent Scales, we provide expert advice on weighbridge selection and site planning, helping businesses find solutions that fit both their operational needs and available space.
